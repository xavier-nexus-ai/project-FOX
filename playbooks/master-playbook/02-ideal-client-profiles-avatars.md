## Purpose

Who FFG and FHL sell to. Why they buy. How each customer gets the right journey.

FFG and FHL sell to the same five humans. The five ICAs below are built from Fox's own settlement data. They are the canonical customer profiles for both businesses. The only difference between FFG and FHL is the purchase: FFG sells asset and personal finance, FHL sells a home-loan purchase type. The ICA-to-FHL mapping further down ties each ICA to its FHL purchase type.

UMI is not in scope.

> **Shared-ICA decision (locked).** There is no separate FHL ICA framework, and none is needed. FFG and FHL share these five ICAs because they serve the same customers. FHL operates a Purchase Type overlay (FHB, New Purchase, Refinance, Investor, Commercial) for its day-to-day work. Each Purchase Type maps back to one of these five ICAs through the ICA-to-FHL Cross-Sell Mapping below. FHL playbooks keep Purchase Type as their operating model and point here for the underlying human profile. They do not redefine the ICA.

## The 5-ICA Framework

Five behavioural profiles cover the FFG customer base. Each one has its own loan footprint, life stage, credit profile, and likely next need. Each gets its own journey, content, FHL pathway, and broker handoff rules.

The framework comes from a conservative probabilistic match against Fox's settlement data. Use it as strong directional segmentation, not customer-level truth.

| # | ICA | One-line description | FHL Pathway |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Young Practical Motor Borrower (YPMB) | Under 40, renting or living at home, first or near-first car loan, light credit file | First Home Buyer |
| 2 | Established Personal Finance Borrower (EPFB) | 30-59, full-time, renting or mortgaged, used a personal loan to solve a life-event need | New Purchase or Refinance |
| 3 | Prime Convenience-Led Repeat Borrower (PCLRB) | 35-60, married or partnered, mortgaged, prime credit, returning customer | New Purchase, Refinance, or Investor |
| 4 | Business Asset Borrower (BAB) | 30-59, self-employed, commercial chattel mortgage, asset-backed | Commercial |
| 5 | Prime Vehicle Borrower (PVB) | 40-59, mortgaged, married/de facto, prime tier vehicle loan | New Purchase, Refinance, or Investor |

### Routing Hierarchy

When a customer matches more than one profile, route in this order (recommended by Rowdie based on commercial value):

1. Business Asset Borrower
2. Prime Vehicle Borrower
3. Young Practical Motor Borrower
4. Prime Convenience-Led Repeat Borrower
5. Established Personal Finance Borrower

## ICA Detail

### ICA 1: Young Practical Motor Borrower (YPMB)

**Who they are:** Under 40. Renting or living at home. Casual, part-time, or full-time. Usually a first-time borrower, maybe one small loan in the past. Credit history is limited or recovering. They came to Fox anxious about approval. Fox came through. That moment of trust is the whole foundation. Lose it at your peril.

| Field | Typical markers |
|---|---|
| App Type | Consumer |
| Loan Type | Consumer Loan (motor / car) |
| Asset | Car |
| Age | Under 30, 30-39 |
| Employment | Mostly full-time, then casual / part-time |
| Residential | Renting or living with parents |
| Relationship | More often single, then de facto |
| Credit | Mixed to weaker-than-prime; broader score spread |
| Financial history | Light file; usually 0 credit cards, 0 personal loans, 0 secured debt |
| Most likely next | Another vehicle loan, or shift into unsecured personal finance |

**What they need from the journey:** Reassurance the loan is working. Education on how this loan is building their financial future. Milestone celebration. A non-intimidating introduction to home ownership as a future possibility.

**Messaging priorities:** Reassurance, affordability, simplicity, speed, confidence.

**FHL pathway:** First Home Buyer. Soft seed at Day 180, Home Ownership Warm-Up at Day 270, direct FHL introduction at Day 365.

**Return loan signal:** Day 365 anniversary message. Their credit has grown. "Your credit has grown - what would you do with a bigger loan?" Personal loan or vehicle upgrade is the natural next step. Debt consolidation is not (they have limited existing debt).

---

### ICA 2: Established Personal Finance Borrower (EPFB)

**Who they are:** 30-59. Full-time employed. Renting or mortgaged. They came to Fox to solve a specific life-event problem. Consolidate debt. Fund a renovation. Pay for a wedding. Take a holiday. They are financially aware. They want clarity, not complexity. They have done their research. They respect professionals who listen.

| Field | Typical markers |
|---|---|
| App Type | Consumer |
| Loan Type | Personal Loan (unsecured) |
| Asset | Other |
| Age | 30-59 |
| Employment | Mostly full-time |
| Residential | Renting or mortgaged |
| Relationship | Mixed single / married / de facto |
| Credit | Mainstream near-prime to solid consumer |
| Financial history | 0-1 credit cards. Often 1+ personal loan. May hold 1 secured loan |
| Most likely next | Another personal loan, debt consolidation, home improvement, planned spending |

**What they need from the journey:** Practical financial education. A sense Fox is working for them, not waiting for the next transaction. Life-event-relevant content. A warm, credible introduction to FHL when timing is right.

**Messaging priorities:** Flexibility, practicality, trust, broad capability, "we can help again".

**FHL pathway:** New Purchase or Refinance. Soft seed at Day 180, direct introduction at Day 270, strong cross-sell at Day 365.

**Return loan signal:** Month 9 onward. After debt consolidation, the natural next is a vehicle purchase or renovation loan once cash flow is freed. After a travel or wedding loan, another life-event personal loan or vehicle upgrade. Do not assume consolidation leads to more consolidation.

---

### ICA 3: Prime Convenience-Led Repeat Borrower (PCLRB)

**Who they are:** 35-60. Married or partnered. Homeowner or mortgaged. Strong credit profile. Well-established credit and lending history. They value speed, simplicity, and being remembered. They want trust plus personal service. They do not need hand-holding. They need to feel like a priority.

**This is FFG's highest-value customer. Treat them accordingly.** Confirmed by Rowdie 26 March 2026: best ROI, fastest settlement, smoothest experience, easiest to create raving fans, unsecured $30K+ loans, decent commission plus lender-funded brokerage fee.

| Field | Typical markers |
|---|---|
| App Type | Consumer |
| Loan Type | Personal Loan (unsecured, prime tier) |
| Loan Tier | A / B (good / great / excellent unsecured) |
| Age | 30-59 |
| Employment | Strongly full-time |
| Residential | Heavily mortgaged, then stable renter |
| Relationship | More often married / de facto |
| Credit | Strongest consumer credit profile. Prime or near-prime |
| Financial history | Controlled and active. 1 credit card common. 0-1 personal loans. Some managed secured exposure |
| Most likely next | Another unsecured loan; fast repeat |

**What they need from the journey:** To feel recognised as a returning or premium customer. VIP treatment. Market intelligence that respects their financial sophistication. Minimal friction when they are ready for the next loan. A compelling reason to think about investment or home equity before they need to.

**Messaging priorities:** Fast-track, loyalty, ease, tailored support, reduced friction. Most professional and peer-level tone of all five ICAs. Less "warm and reassuring", more "I know your situation, here is what I'm watching".

**FHL pathway:** New Purchase, Refinance, or Investor. Highest FHL conversion potential of any ICA. Tailor the FHL seed at Day 180 based on whether they already own a home.

**Return loan signal:** Highest probability of any ICA. Pre-approval offer at Day 270 is the most effective trigger. If no return loan by Month 9, include a direct "what's your next purchase?" SMS. Their return is driven by life events and opportunity, not financial stress.

### ICA 4: Business Asset Borrower (BAB)

**Who they are:** 30-59. Self-employed. Mortgaged or renting. Married or de facto. They run a small business (3-10+ years trading) in trades, construction, transport, or professional services. They are shifting from "survive and hustle" to "grow and systemise". They have used finance before but as one-off fixes. They have had mixed experiences with banks who don't understand self-employed income, trusts, or company structures. They want a finance partner who gets it.

| Field | Typical markers |
|---|---|
| App Type | Commercial |
| Loan Type | Chattel Mortgage |
| Asset | Mostly car, then equipment, trailer, other business-use |
| Age | 30-59 |
| Employment | Overwhelmingly self-employed |
| Residential | Often mortgaged, then renting |
| Relationship | More often married / de facto |
| Credit | Stronger than distressed consumer but more variable. Lite-doc / asset-backed logic matters more than pure score |
| Financial history | Light personal loan history. 0 personal loans. 0-1 credit cards |
| Most likely next | Another commercial asset loan. Replacement vehicle / equipment. Expansion asset |

**What they need from the journey:** Recognition Fox understands self-employed income. Joined-up finance strategy across vehicles, equipment, and cash flow. Tax-timing intelligence. EOFY prompts. Support for property goals alongside business growth.

**Messaging priorities:** Commercial outcomes, tax timing, asset upgrade, growth support, preserve working capital.

**Loan amount:** Typically $100,000 - $200,000 in total facilities, often a mix of 2-4 vehicles plus equipment plus a cash-flow line of credit. Scale potential as the business grows.

**FHL pathway:** Commercial. Property appetite is real (owner-occupier upgrade plus investment property). Treat them as a multi-product, long-term client.

**Return loan signal:** EOFY. Replacement cycle. Growth event. Additional vehicle or equipment.

---

### ICA 5: Prime Vehicle Borrower (PVB)

**Who they are:** 40-59. Mortgaged. Married or de facto. Established household. Stronger credit than the younger motor cohort. Clearly prime tier. They are not just trying to get a car loan. They expect a smoother, cleaner, more efficient borrowing experience.

| Field | Typical markers |
|---|---|
| App Type | Consumer |
| Loan Type | Consumer Loan (car, prime tier) |
| Asset | Car |
| Age | 40-49 and 50-59 |
| Employment | Mainly full-time |
| Residential | Mainly mortgaged, then renting |
| Relationship | More often married, then de facto |
| Credit | Clearly prime tier. Expects certainty and smooth execution |
| Financial history | More active but controlled. 1 credit card common. 0 personal loans typical. Some secured exposure |
| Most likely next | Another vehicle, upgrade or replacement, or shift into broader personal finance |

**What they need from the journey:** Ease, certainty, quality of experience. Efficient process. A trusted relationship that picks up where it left off.

**Messaging priorities:** Ease, certainty, quality of experience, efficient process, trusted relationship.

**FHL pathway:** New Purchase, Refinance, or Investor. Property equity may be working harder elsewhere; raise the question.

**Return loan signal:** Upgrade cycle. Replacement timing. Household vehicle change. Convenience-led borrowing.

### FHL Writing Guidance by Purchase Type

| Purchase Type | Writing focus |
|---|---|
| First Home Buyer | Fundamentals. Deposit and LVR basics. Up-front costs. Pre-approval flow. Confidence-building next steps |
| New Purchase | Readiness. Borrowing power. Offer / contract timing. Pre-approval. Reducing friction |

| Investor | Cash flow language. Long-term strategy. Structure / features. Risk management WITHOUT giving advice |
| Commercial | Documentation. Timelines. Deal structure variables. Clear "what to prepare" checklists |

## UMI (Out of Scope)

UMI Loans is part of the Fox Group ecosystem but **not in scope for this playbook**. No UMI ICA. No UMI journey. No UMI cross-sell logic.

The only UMI thing that matters for this build is one guardrail. If a UMI client ever appears in FFG or FHL contact lists (because they graduated up, or because data sources cross over), the system needs to know which UMI clients are "direct" and which are "broker-referred". Broker-referred UMI clients cannot receive FFG or FHL cross-sell communications. Direct UMI clients can.

**Action:** Apply a `UMI_BrokerReferred` tag to broker-sourced UMI contacts in GHL. Suppress them from any FFG or FHL nurture audience. Direct UMI clients are tagged `UMI_Direct` and are eligible. That eligibility is not built in this playbook.

That is the entire UMI footprint in this playbook.
