### Cultural Communication Style

- **Directness:** Australian direct. Say what we mean without being blunt. No beating around the bush, but no aggression either.
- **Formality:** Generally casual. First names from the start. "Hi" not "Dear". "Cheers" not "Kind regards".
- **Humour:** Self-deprecating and understated. Never forced. A light touch works (e.g., "We don't bite" or "a team that genuinely gives a damn (sorry, we do)").
- **Cultural references:** Local and Queensland-relevant. Avoid US-centric examples. Reference Australian life (cars, property market, RBA rates, local suburbs).
- **Egalitarian tone:** No talking down. The broker and the client are equals having a conversation.

### Date & Number Formats

- **Dates:** DD/MM/YYYY (e.g., 05/03/2026)
- **Currency:** AUD (e.g., $1,500.00). No need to specify "AUD" unless international context.
- **Phone:** Australian format (e.g., 07 XXXX XXXX or 04XX XXX XXX)
- **Time:** 12-hour format with am/pm (e.g., 2:30 pm)

## 3. Voice Overview

### Brand DNA: The Financial Companion

Fox Finance Group exists at a specific intersection in financial services: knowledgeable enough to be trusted, approachable enough to be comfortable, and honest enough to be believed.

Our voice reflects a financial companion who walks beside clients rather than lectures from above.

### Brand Personality

If Fox Finance Group walked into a room, they would be the person who makes everyone feel less stressed about money. They would be the knowledgeable friend at a barbecue who explains home loans over a beer, without making you feel stupid for asking.

**Personality Adjectives:** Clear, Calm, Empowering, Human, Grounded

### Brand Archetype

**Primary Archetype:** The Guide (wise, supportive, trustworthy)
**Secondary Archetype:** The Companion (walks beside, not ahead)

### StoryBrand Position

- **Hero:** The customer. People sorting out finance for cars, homes, equipment, or rebuilding their credit.
- **Guide:** Fox Finance Group. The knowledgeable companion who makes finance less stressful.
- **Problem:** Finance feels overwhelming, jargon-heavy, and high-pressure. People do not know who to trust.
- **Plan:** We listen first, explain in plain English, find the right fit, and keep you in the loop.
- **Success:** Finance is sorted. You feel confident, informed, and in control of your decisions.
- **Failure:** You end up with the wrong product, overpay, or feel pressured into something that does not suit you.

### Tone Spectrum

Fox's tone adjusts based on context but always stays within these boundaries:

```
[Casual] ------[X]--|-------------- [Formal]
[Playful] -----|---[X]------------- [Serious]
[Humble] ------|---------[X]------- [Authoritative]
[Simple] ---[X]|------------------- [Technical]
[Warm] ----[X]-|------------------- [Clinical]
```

We sit firmly on the casual, simple, and warm side. We lean slightly toward authoritative (we know our stuff) but never lose the human touch.

## 4. Voice Characteristics

### We Sound Like...

| Characteristic | Description | Example |
|---------------|-------------|--------|
| **Clear** | We explain finance in plain English. No jargon. If a Year 8 student could not understand it, rewrite it. | "Here's how your repayments break down over the next 12 months" |
| **Calm** | We lower blood pressure, not raise it. We remove urgency and add certainty. | "We'll walk you through each step so you know exactly what's happening" |
| **Empowering** | We present options and consequences. The customer decides. | "Here are your options, and here's what each one means for you" |
| **Human** | We sound like a knowledgeable friend, not a faceless institution. | "We know buying a car shouldn't feel this complicated" |
| **Optimistic** | We are positive about outcomes without making promises we cannot keep. | "Most people in your situation find they're in a much better position within 12 months" |
| **Low-pressure** | We never push. The door is always open. Timing is up to them. | "No rush, but we're here when you're ready" |

### We NEVER Sound Like...

| Anti-characteristic | Why We Avoid It | Example to Avoid |
|--------------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Pushy** | Finance decisions are personal. Pressure destroys trust. | "Act now before rates change! Don't miss this opportunity!" |
| **Corporate** | Formal language creates distance. We want closeness. | "Fox Finance Group endeavours to simplify the vehicular acquisition process" |
| **Jargon-heavy** | If they need a dictionary, we have failed. | "The amortisation schedule reflects a declining balance methodology across the term" |
| **Uncertain** | Vagueness erodes confidence. Be clear about what we know and do not know. | "Results may vary based on individual circumstances and market conditions" |
| **Robotic** | Every message should feel like a real person wrote it. | "Dear valued customer, your application reference #48291 has been processed" |
| **Condescending** | We walk beside, not above. Never talk down. | "As you may not be aware, interest rates work by..." |

| Category | Avoided Words | Why |

| **Pressure words** | act now, limited time, don't miss out, exclusive offer, urgent | Too pushy. We never create false urgency. |
| **Complex words** | endeavour, ascertain, commence, facilitate, pursuant | Too formal. Use everyday words. |
| **Distancing language** | valued customer, dear sir/madam, to whom it may concern | Too impersonal. Use their name. |

|--------------|----------------|

| Forbidden | Why | Use Instead |
|-----------|-----|-------------|

**Tier 1 (Absolute Ban):** delve, tapestry, embark, realm, leverage, navigate, unlock, landscape, foster, robust, synergy, beacon

## 6. Grammatical Patterns

### Preferred Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Why |
|---------|---------|-----|
| **Active voice** | "We'll review your application" not "Your application will be reviewed" | Sounds more direct and human |
| **Second person** | "You can..." not "Customers can..." | This is about them, not us |
| **Present tense** | "Here's what happens next" not "What will happen is..." | More immediate and clear |
| **Contractions** | "We'll", "You're", "It's", "Don't" | Sounds natural and conversational |
| **Short declaratives** | "No pressure. Just a conversation." | Creates rhythm and emphasis |
| **Questions to engage** | "Sound familiar?" or "Not sure which one fits?" | Draws the reader in |

### Patterns to Avoid

| Pattern | Example | Why |
|---------|---------|-----|
| **Passive voice** | "Mistakes were made" or "Your application will be processed" | Sounds evasive and corporate |
| **Nominalisations** | "The implementation of..." instead of "When we implement..." | Too formal and wordy |
| **Third person self-reference** | "Fox Finance Group believes..." | Sounds corporate. Say "We believe..." |
| **Formal address** | "Dear Mr Smith" or "To whom it may concern" | Too stiff. Use "Hi {first_name}" |
| **Hedging language** | "We would like to suggest that perhaps..." | Sounds uncertain. Be direct. |

## 7. Punctuation

### Preferences

| Punctuation | Usage | Example |
|-------------|-------|--------|
| **Full stops** | Short sentences. Clear and direct. One idea per sentence. | "Your application is in. We're on it." |
| **Commas** | Use naturally. No Oxford comma unless needed for clarity. | "cars, equipment and home loans" |
| **Exclamation marks** | Sparingly. Max one per email. Only for genuine celebration. | "Great news, you're approved!" |
| **Question marks** | Use to engage the reader and create conversation. | "Not sure which one fits? That's literally what we're here for." |
| **Ellipsis** | Rarely. Can work for trailing thoughts in casual contexts. | "Whether that's tomorrow, next month, or next year..." |
| **EM dashes** | **NEVER USE.** Use commas, full stops, or parentheses instead. | Use commas or full stops to break up ideas. |
| **Semicolons** | Avoid. Use two sentences instead. | Split into two clear sentences. |
| **Colons** | Fine for introducing lists or explanations. | "Here's what happens next:" |

## 8. Sentence Structure

### Preferred Patterns

- **Average sentence length:** 12-18 words
- **Maximum sentence length:** 25 words
- **Short sentence percentage:** 30% of sentences should be under 8 words
- **Preferred structure:** Lead with the benefit, then explain

### Examples

**Good (matches voice):**
> "Your finance has been approved. Here's what happens next."

> "No complicated forms. No pressure. Just a conversation about what works for you."

> "We get it. Finance decisions take time, and that's totally fine."

**Bad (does not match voice):**
> "We are pleased to inform you that your application for finance has been reviewed and subsequently approved by the relevant lending institution."

> "Our comprehensive suite of financial products and services is designed to cater to a wide range of customer needs across multiple market segments."

### Paragraph Length

- **Ideal:** 2-3 sentences per paragraph
- **Maximum:** 4 sentences
- **One-line paragraphs:** Yes, for emphasis. "We've got this." or "No stress."
- **Email length:** Under 200 words where possible

## 9. Rhetorical Devices

### Devices We Use

| Device | Example | When to Use |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Direct questions** | "Sound familiar?" / "Not sure which one fits?" | To engage the reader and create dialogue |
| **Lists of three** | "No pressure. No judgement. No time limit." | To create rhythm and emphasis |
| **Social proof** | "Here's what our clients say..." | To build trust without self-promotion |
| **Plain metaphors** | "The finish line is in sight" | To make progress tangible |
| **Conversational asides** | "(sorry, we do)" or "(fair enough)" | To add personality and warmth |
| **Direct address** | "You relax. We've got this." | To reassure and create partnership |
| **Understatement** | "Quick heads up" / "Just a quick one" | Australian-style casual framing |

### Devices We Avoid

| Device | Why |
|--------|-----|
| **Hyperbole** | "The best finance experience ever!" overpromises and undermines trust |
| **Sarcasm** | Can be misread in written form. Keep warmth genuine. |
| **Fear-based urgency** | "Miss this and you'll regret it!" is the opposite of calm and reassuring |
| **Corporate buzzwords** | "Synergistic solutions" means nothing to real people |
| **Excessive superlatives** | "Amazing, incredible, revolutionary" sounds like AI content |

## 10. Paragraph & Content Structure

### Email Structure

| Component | Guideline |
|---------|----------|
| **Subject line** | Clear benefit, under 50 characters. Can include one emoji for brand recognition. |
| **Opening** | "Hi {first_name}," then straight to the point. Warm but efficient. |
| **Body** | One key message per email. Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences). Use bullets and numbered lists to break down steps. |
| **Closing** | Warm sign-off with a clear next step. "Cheers," is our standard. |
| **Signature** | Broker's first name + Fox Finance Group / Fox Home Loans / UMI Loans. |
| **CTA** | One per email. Action-oriented. "Check your options" not "Click here". |

### General Content Structure

- **Headlines:** Benefit-led, under 10 words. Plain language.
- **Subheadings:** Every 2-3 paragraphs. Questions work well ("What happens next?").
- **Bullet points:** Max 5 items per list. Start with the most important.
- **CTAs:** Action-oriented, specific, one per content block. Use conversational language.
- **Numbered steps:** For processes. People like knowing where they are in a sequence.
