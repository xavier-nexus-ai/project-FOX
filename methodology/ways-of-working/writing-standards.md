# Writing Standards

All written output from the Njin PM Framework must follow these standards. They apply to agent output, PM communications, client-facing documents, and internal notes.

---

## Language & Locale

- **Australian English** — colour, organise, behaviour, specialise, programme (except computer program)
- **Currency:** AUD ($) unless explicitly stated otherwise
- **Timezone:** AEST (Australia/Brisbane, UTC+10)
- **Date format:** YYYY-MM-DD for technical files, natural format for prose ("8 April 2026")

---

## Tone & Voice

**Direct, punchy, results-obsessed.**

- Lead with the answer or action, not the reasoning
- Don't restate what was said — just respond
- Short sentences where they land harder
- Tables over walls of text
- Never hedge. Say what you mean.

**For client communications:**
- Third-grade reading level — genius ten-year-old energy
- Think at the top 0.001% but write so anyone can follow
- Use bullet points and numbered lists
- Get to the point fast
- Simple words, short sentences
- If it sounds like an AI wrote it, rewrite it

---

## Formatting Rules

### What to use:
- **Bullet points** for lists of 3+ items
- **Tables** for comparisons, decision matrices, data
- **Headings** to create scannable structure
- **Bold** for key terms and decisions
- **Code blocks** for file paths, commands, templates

### What NOT to use:
- **Em dashes (—)** — NEVER in client comms. Rare in internal docs.
- **Walls of text** — if you can't scan it in 10 seconds, restructure it
- **Corporate jargon** — see anti-pattern list below
- **Emojis** — unless explicitly requested by the client or in a casual channel

---

## Words to Never Use

These words signal generic AI output or corporate fluff. If you catch yourself writing them, rewrite:

- leverage
- synergy
- holistic
- robust
- navigate
- tapestry
- treasure trove
- delve
- unveil
- unlock
- pitfalls
- comprehensive
- multifaceted
- paradigm shift
- ecosystem (unless literal)
- cutting-edge
- revolutionary
- game-changing
- seamless
- streamlined

**Rule:** Rewrite any sentence containing these words before shipping.

## Tone by Audience

### Internal (James, team)
- Direct, no fluff
- Call out problems plainly
- Disagree when warranted — make the case once, then execute
- Short sentences, tables, bullet points
- Use "we" for team decisions, "I" for opinions

### George (co-founder)
- Collaborative, not directive
- "I need your help" not "I need you to do this"
- Requests, not orders
- He's a partner, treat him as an equal

### Team members (Xavier, Michael, John, Joe, Sheena)
- Direct is fine
- Specific tasks with specific outcomes
- No vague guidance
- Always end with a clear next step

### Clients
- Professional but warm
- Simple language (third-grade level)
- No jargon
- Frame deliverables as outcomes, not features
- Always specify next steps and timelines

## Structure for Common Outputs

### Status updates
1. What's done
2. What's in flight
3. What's blocked (if anything)
4. What's next

### Client sprint reports
1. Re-sell the decision (why they bought)
2. Results delivered this sprint
3. ROI to date
4. Next sprint preview
5. Seed next-stage upsell (soft)

### Internal decisions
1. The decision
2. The reason (short)
3. What changes because of this
4. Who's doing what next

### Problem escalations
1. The problem (1 sentence)
2. The impact (1 sentence)
3. The options (2-3 max)
4. The recommendation

## Voice Scoring

For any output meant to match a client's brand voice, score against their Tone of Voice Guide before shipping. The voice-scoring skill handles this — use it for:
- Playbook copy
- Client-facing emails
- Presentations
- Marketing materials

Never ship client-branded content without voice scoring.

---

## Client-Specific Voice

Every Njin Method engagement produces a Tone of Voice Guide during Activation. This is a **blocking gate** — no client copy is generated until the guide is approved.

The guide includes:
- Vocabulary (words they use, words they avoid)
- Sentence patterns
- Personality traits
- Examples of on-voice and off-voice writing
