# Framework Reference: Njin Method

## Purpose

The Njin Method is a revenue-first consulting framework that diagnoses where a business is leaking money, then delivers AI-powered playbooks to plug those leaks. Technology is the implementation method — revenue impact is always the starting point.

---

## Target Audience

**Primary:** Mid-market B2B businesses with active sales teams, an existing CRM, and a clear revenue constraint they haven't been able to solve internally. Typically $2M–$50M revenue, 3–30 staff, running some form of outbound or inbound lead generation.

**Client signals that indicate this framework fits:**
- "We're getting leads but they're going cold before they book"
- "Our close rate is lower than it should be"
- "We don't have a systematic referral or retention process"
- "We're running ads but don't know what's working"
- Revenue is flat or growing slowly despite reasonable lead volume
- The business has a sales team but no documented process
- CRM exists but is not being used consistently

---

## Phases / Methodology

The engagement follows four phases. The first two establish foundations; the second two are repeatable per playbook.

### Phase 1: Qualification

Collect the 12 Core Numbers and run constraint diagnosis. Identify which revenue pillar (Acquisition, Conversion, Monetisation, Retention) is the primary leak. Propose the right offer package.

### Phase 2: Activation

Complete data collection and validate access. Create the Tone of Voice Guide — a non-negotiable blocking gate before any content is created. Confirm playbook selection and lock down CRM access.

### Phase 3: Implementation (12 weeks per playbook)

- **Week 1:** Immersion — deep dive into the business, team, existing processes
- **Weeks 1–2:** Methodology creation — build the playbook document (20 sections)
- **Week 3:** Strategic approval — client reviews and signs off on playbook
- **Weeks 4–6:** CRM implementation — build automations, pipelines, workflows
- **Weeks 7–8:** Team training — SDRs, closers, CSMs trained on the system
- **Weeks 9–12:** Live monitoring — watch performance, tune, report

### Phase 4: Ongoing Success

Weekly health checks, monthly strategic reviews, quarterly business reviews. Identify the next constraint once the first playbook is producing results. Secondary constraint becomes the next playbook.

## Key Deliverables

The client ends the engagement with:

- **Playbook document** — 20-section master playbook specific to their constraint (Pre-Sales, Sales, Outreach, Ads, Retention, Cross-Sell, or Referral)
- **Developer Guide** — technical blueprint for CRM implementation, extracted from Section 7 of the playbook
- **Role-specific Handbooks** — operational guides per sales role (SDR, Closer, CSM), extracted from Section 8
- **Scripts library** — all email, SMS, call, and LinkedIn templates from Section 10
- **Quick Reference Guide** — 1–2 page cheat sheet for reps
- **Template library** — all copy-paste templates in a separate doc
- **Implementation checklist** — step-by-step go-live guide
- **Configured CRM** — pipelines, automations, and workflows built and tested
- **Trained team** — reps certified on the new system
- **Before/after metrics** — Core 12 updated with actual results

## Blocking Gates

Gates prevent premature progression. All conditions must be met before moving forward.

| Gate | Required Conditions |
|------|---------------------|
| **Activation Gate** | CRM admin access granted, historical data received, team interviews scheduled, Tone of Voice Guide complete, Core 12 logged |
| **Playbook Approval Gate** | Client has reviewed and signed off on the playbook document before any CRM build begins |
| **Go-Live Gate** | All automations tested end-to-end, team trained and certified, manager has Call Review Framework, dashboards configured |

**The Data Access Gate is firm.** No CRM access means the implementation clock does not start. Escalate after 5 business days with no access and document the blocker in the COS.

**The Tone of Voice Gate is firm.** No content — scripts, emails, SMS, copy — gets created until the Tone of Voice Guide is complete and client-approved.

## How to Scope This Framework

Before scoping, a PM needs to know:

1. **The Core 12 Numbers** — Revenue (current and prior year), leads/mo, cost per lead, lead-to-appointment rate, show rate, close rate, average deal size, MRR/contract length, churn, LTV, CAC. These determine which pillar is leaking and which playbook to run first.
2. **Primary constraint** — Acquisition, Conversion, Monetisation, or Retention. Constraint diagnosis drives everything.
3. **CRM platform** — GoHighLevel, HubSpot, Salesforce, or other. Determines implementation complexity and which automations are possible.
4. **Team composition** — How many reps, what roles (SDR, Closer, CSM). Determines which role-specific handbooks and training to create.
5. **Playbook selection** — Which playbook type is right for the constraint? One playbook per 12-week cycle. Do not run two playbooks in parallel.
6. **Existing materials** — What does the client already have? Scripts, brand guidelines, case studies, ad copy. These become knowledge base inputs.

**Common scoping pitfalls:**
- Agreeing to start before CRM access is confirmed
- Not locking down the playbook type before starting content
- Scoping two playbooks to run simultaneously — the framework is sequential by design
- Underestimating time to get Tone of Voice approved when the client doesn't have existing brand guidelines

## Signals This is the Right Framework

- Client has a functioning business with real revenue but a clear, diagnosable constraint
- They have a sales team (or plan to build one) and want a documented process
- They use a CRM (or are willing to adopt one)
- Their problem falls clearly into one of the four revenue pillars
- They want a tangible, documented playbook they own — not just strategic advice
- They're willing to give their team 2–4 weeks of focused time for training and go-live
- Revenue is between $1M and $50M (below this, the system may be over-engineered; above this, they likely need the Revenue Accelerator or Ultimate Growth Plan)

---

## Signals This is NOT the Right Framework

- The business has no existing revenue or is pre-product — route to strategy consulting first
- The problem is primarily a product or market-fit issue, not a revenue system issue
- The client wants a custom AI build (an agent, chatbot, or automation tool) without a playbook context — consider a standalone AI build scoped separately
- The client has structured IP, frameworks, or a proprietary methodology they want to turn into an AI system — route to **AI Orchestration (90-Day AI System Build)** instead
- The client's primary constraint is operational (fulfilment, delivery, team) rather than revenue — the Njin Method won't address this directly
