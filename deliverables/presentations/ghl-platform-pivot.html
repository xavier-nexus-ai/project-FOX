04 · Known Limits

What GHL doesn't do well.

Being upfront. GHL nails 90% of what the playbooks need. Here's the 10% that requires workarounds, so there are no surprises later.

!

Relational data between contacts

Biggest Limit

GHL's contact model is flat. You can't cleanly link two contacts and attach a shared record between them — the sort of thing you hit with referrers, joint borrowers, introducers, partner-to-client relationships. We saw this pain first-hand in a recent referral playbook build.

How we handle it

Known workarounds: custom fields that mirror the link, tagging conventions, and opportunity objects to carry shared context. It works — it's just hand-rolled, not elegant.

!

Cross-brand data between FFG, FHL and UMI

Because the relational model is flat, the cross-brand view — one customer holding products across all three brands — won't be native. It will be hacked together using conventions rather than structure.

How we handle it

Single GHL account with disciplined tagging and custom fields to carry the brand signal. Cross-sell triggers fire from those. It's durable, just not what a purpose-built cross-brand system would look like.

!

Complex scoring and reporting

Native scoring and cross-brand reporting is basic. Rich models and bespoke dashboards aren't GHL's strength.

How we handle it

Start with rule-based triggers and GHL reporting. If Fox needs deeper analytics, a lightweight external layer can be added later without disturbing the playbook.
